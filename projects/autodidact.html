AUTODIDACT: a self-improving robot learning loop

An autonomous learning loop for a simulated cath-lab robot that finds gaps in its own training data, asks for targeted demonstrations, retrains, and promotes a new policy only when a statistical test shows it is better.

Problem and motivation

Robot policies learned from demonstrations are only as good as the data behind them. Finding out where a policy is weak, collecting the right new demonstrations, and deciding whether a retrained model is actually better are usually manual, slow and hard to audit.

In a clinical setting such as a cath lab, every model update also has to be safe and traceable. The challenge was to close that loop autonomously: let the system improve itself while keeping each decision explainable and the robot inside its safety constraints.

System architecture

The system runs against a MuJoCo digital twin of the FR3 Duo. Around the learned policy sit the components that make the loop autonomous and auditable: data validation and failure analysis, retraining, a statistical promotion gate, a safety filter between the policy and the robot, a decision ledger and a live dashboard.

Learning-loop workflow

Each cycle follows the same sequence. The diagram below is a conceptual summary of the loop as described in the project.

  1. Identify gapsFind where the training data does not cover what the policy needs.
  2. Request demonstrationsAsk for targeted demonstrations for those gaps.
  3. Validate dataCheck incoming data before it is used for training.
  4. Retrain policyTrain a candidate policy on the extended dataset.
  5. Evaluate statisticallyCompare candidate and current policy with a statistical test.
  6. Promote if betterReplace the current policy only when the test shows an improvement.

My contributions

AUTODIDACT was a five-person team effort. My part focused on the reliability, safety and traceability of the loop:

  • Data validation and failure-analysis tooling to check demonstration data and understand why runs failed.
  • A safety filter with stop-and-resume that halts motion when the robot approaches a keep-out zone and resumes safely afterwards.
  • A decision ledger that logs every model-update decision, so each policy change can be traced.
  • Contributed to the live dashboard showing data, robot runs and system decisions.

Safety and validation

Three mechanisms keep the loop safe and accountable:

  • Safety filter. Sits between the policy and the robot and stops motion that would enter a keep-out zone, then resumes.
  • Statistical promotion gate. A retrained policy only replaces the current one when a statistical test shows it is better, so the system cannot drift to a worse model on a lucky run.
  • Decision ledger. Every model update is logged, making the system's choices auditable after the fact.

Lessons learned

  • Treat safety as its own layer. A filter that sits outside the learned policy can be tested and measured on its own, independent of how the policy changes.
  • Autonomy needs a clear acceptance rule. Promoting a model only after a statistical test turns "seems better" into a decision the system can make on its own.
  • Traceability builds trust. Logging each decision made it possible to explain what the system did and why, which matters for medical robotics.
  • Scope hard under time pressure. Forty-eight hours forced the team to agree early on a loop that could run end to end, then improve it.
