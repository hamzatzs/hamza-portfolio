Bat-inspired micro UAV

A biologically inspired micro aerial vehicle for potential disaster-response and surveillance applications, built around a wing mechanism that flaps, morphs and twists.

Type
Final-year project
Degree
B.Sc. Mechanical Engineering, GIKI
Programme
2019–2023
Disciplines
Design, simulation, control, testing
  1. Motivation
  2. Wing mechanism
  3. Simulation and analysis
  4. Control system
  5. Prototyping and testing
  6. Takeaways

Motivation

Bats change the shape of their wings throughout every wingbeat, which gives them agility that fixed-wing and rotary micro air vehicles struggle to match. The project set out to capture aspects of that motion in a small vehicle suited to tasks such as disaster response and surveillance.

Wing mechanism

The core of the design is a novel four-degree-of-freedom wing mechanism, modelled in SolidWorks, that combines three motions:

  • Flapping, the up-and-down stroke that generates lift and thrust.
  • Morphing, changing the wing's shape and area during the stroke.
  • Twisting, changing the wing's angle along its span.

Lightweight components were produced by 3D printing to keep the vehicle's mass low.

Simulation and analysis

The design was analysed before and alongside building:

  • CFD and FEA in ANSYS, COMSOL and MATLAB to study aerodynamics, structural integrity and energy efficiency.
  • Multibody dynamics in MSC Adams to simulate the wing's motion.

Control system

A microcontroller-based control system (Arduino / Raspberry Pi) adjusts the wing in real time, linking the mechanical design to its actuation.

Prototyping and testing

A scaled prototype was built and validated through wind-tunnel and field testing.

Takeaways

  • Design, analysis and build in one loop. The project ran from CAD through CFD, FEA and multibody simulation to a tested prototype.
  • Mechanics and control are coupled. A multi-degree-of-freedom wing only works when the mechanism and its real-time control are designed together.
  • Test what you simulate. Wind-tunnel and field tests were the check on what the simulations predicted.
